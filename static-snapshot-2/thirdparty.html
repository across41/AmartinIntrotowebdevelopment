What are 3rd party cookies, and why do we even have them?

Third-Party Cookies are cookies that are set by a website and keep track of your cookies when you jump back and forth between websites. Which is much different than first party cookie. First party cookies are cookies that are only tracked on the specific domain you are on; they help keep your shopping cart and remember certain things you select. While first party cookies do exist, they are hardly ever used anymore. The best way to describe Third-Party Cookies is like “trail of crumbs” as mentioned in the article by cookiescript.com. When you are a user browsing through a webpage every tab you have open creates a crumb on the trail and when you move on to the next page the trail is still there.

So as you move on from each page you are relaying information into other websites in order for them to push certain ads or marketing to you. They not only are able to do this through just remembering certain websites you have visited you can also, use a website where you may have used your email to create a log in and the cookies were actually able to store your data so later you may have gotten an email for a coupon for something you looked up hours before. Cookies are something that most users can disable by clearing the cookies and cache so third parties are not able to track what you are visiting, but lots of people find them helpful as they are able to get ads from companies for products and are able to find more of what they are looking for.

Is it marketing or is it invasive?

There is a arugement to be had on whether Third-Party cookies are actually a good thing or if they really just are a huge invasion of people privacy. Some people love the true convenience of third party ads. It has allowed for businesses to thrive as they are able to push thier products to people that were looking for a similar product. While it does have some benefits, people are truly concerned for thier secruity as many times like most of us, we go shop on Amazon buy something we need, the we go and log into our bank account or our retirement funds and the whole time these third party cookies were running in the background.

Many businesses are started to be forced into blocking Third-Party cookies. Accoring to easyinsights.ai "Laws like GDPR and CCPA have led to browsers like Safari and Firefox blocking third party cookies." Many other companies like Google and Apple are also finally getting on the train of blocking Third-Party cookies. This year we have already started to see the fall of these Third-Party cookies with most of these large web companies starting to restrict them.

Like everything, there will be some negative effects that take place with Third-Party cookies leaving. It will be difficult for marketers to be able to push personalized ads to people or track what customers are doing after they are no longer on that sight. Companies will have to start getting more and more creative on tracking and reporting to being able to track what the customers need in order to drive up engagement.

While the hope is that the demise of Third-Party cookies will lead to a better and safer web, there is no guarentee that this may not lead to the downfall of some businesses.
